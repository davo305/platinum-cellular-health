RESEARCH FOUNDATIONS

Core laboratory terminology

Clear terminology helps prevent confusion between analytical identity, reported purity, sample concentration, and lot-specific documentation.

These concepts are especially important when reviewing testing reports and comparing records from different samples or laboratories.

Identity Whether analytical evidence supports the stated chemical or molecular identity of a research sample.
Purity A reported analytical measurement describing the proportion of a detected target component relative to other detected components under the stated testing method.
Lot / Batch Number An identifier used to connect a specific production or research batch with its documentation, testing records, and traceability information.
Sample Identifier The identifier assigned to a specific submitted sample by a laboratory or submitting organization.
Analytical Method The testing technique or procedure used to generate a reported analytical result.
ANALYTICAL SCIENCE

Different tests answer different questions.

IDENTITY

Identity Testing

Identity-focused analysis is used to evaluate whether analytical evidence is consistent with the stated research compound.

PURITY

Purity Analysis

Purity-focused testing evaluates the relative composition of detected components under the analytical method used.

REPORTING

Method Context

Reported values should be interpreted together with the analytical method, sample preparation, and reporting conventions used by the laboratory.

TRACEABILITY

Lot Matching

Testing documentation is most useful when the report can be matched to the specific sample, lot, or batch being referenced.

LABORATORY PRACTICE

General storage and documentation concepts

Laboratory materials should be handled according to their specific manufacturer, supplier, institutional, or analytical requirements. General research practice also emphasizes labeling, environmental control, and traceability.

Temperature: Maintain storage conditions appropriate to the specific research material and its documentation.
Light Exposure: Some laboratory materials may require protection from direct or prolonged light exposure.
Moisture: Control moisture exposure when required by the material's documented storage conditions.
Labeling: Use clear labels that preserve sample identity, lot or batch information, and relevant dates.
Traceability: Keep documentation associated with the specific research material being referenced.
