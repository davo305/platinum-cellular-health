RESEARCH FOUNDATIONS

Core laboratory terminology.

Understanding analytical language helps distinguish between the identity of a material, reported purity, quantity, batch information, and the specific sample that was actually tested.

Identity

Whether analytical evidence is consistent with the stated chemical or molecular identity of the sample.

Purity

A laboratory-reported measurement describing the relative proportion of a detected target component under the stated analytical method.

Quantity / Content

The amount of material reported by the analytical laboratory for the tested sample under its stated method.

Lot / Batch

An identifier used to connect a production or research batch with documentation and testing records.

Sample Identifier

A laboratory or submitter-assigned identifier used to distinguish one tested sample from another.

Reference Standard

A characterized material used by a laboratory as a comparison point during certain analytical procedures.

ANALYTICAL SCIENCE

Different tests answer different questions.

A single analytical result does not necessarily describe every property of a research sample. Understanding the purpose of each method helps put laboratory results into the correct context.

IDENTITY

Identity Analysis

Used to evaluate whether analytical evidence is consistent with the stated target material.

PURITY

Chromatographic Purity

Evaluates detected components under a stated chromatographic method and reporting system.

QUANTITY

Content Analysis

Used to report the measured amount of material within the tested analytical sample.

MICROBIOLOGY

Sterility / Endotoxin

Separate analytical or microbiological assessments that address questions different from identity or purity.

DOCUMENTATION

How to read a Certificate of Analysis.

A Certificate of Analysis is best understood as a report connected to a specific submitted sample, lot, batch, or laboratory identifier.

Results should be interpreted together with the test method, report date, sample identity, and any laboratory notes or limitations included on the original document.

Batch or Lot Number Connects the report to the batch or lot identified by the submitter or laboratory.
Laboratory / Task ID Provides a report or sample reference that can help distinguish one analytical submission from another.
Reported Result May include purity, quantity, identity, sterility, endotoxin, or other measurements.
Analysis / Report Date Shows when testing or reporting occurred, depending on the laboratory's documentation format.
LABORATORY PRACTICE

General storage and handling principles.

Specific storage requirements depend on the material and its applicable documentation. These are general laboratory concepts rather than product-specific instructions.

Temperature Control

Maintain environmental conditions appropriate to the specific material and the storage documentation that applies to it.

Light Exposure

Some laboratory materials may require protection from direct or prolonged light exposure.

Moisture Control

Control moisture exposure when required by the material's documented storage conditions.

Clear Labeling

Preserve sample identity, amount, lot or batch information, and relevant dates where applicable.

Traceability

Keep analytical reports and related records connected to the specific material they reference.

Original Documentation

When available, retain the original analytical report rather than relying only on summarized results.
